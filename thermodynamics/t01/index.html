Latent Heats of Water Phase Changes

USTHB calorimetry practical for determining the latent heat of steam condensation/vaporization and the latent heat of ice fusion.

Two distinct experiments

The published USTHB procedure determines the latent heat of water vaporization from steam condensed into cold water, then determines the latent heat of ice fusion from dry ice cubes added to warm water. Both rely on an insulated calorimeter and conservation of energy.

ΣQᵢ = 0

Use cp,water = 4.18 kJ·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹. Steam is taken to enter as saturated steam at 100°C and ice as dry ice at 0°C.

Measurements required by the practical

  1. Measure ambient temperature Ta.
  2. Weigh the empty calorimeter Mcal.
  3. Weigh calorimeter + initial water and measure Ti.
  4. Carry out the steam-condensation or ice-melting operation while stirring continuously.
  5. Measure equilibrium Tf, then immediately weigh the final calorimeter Mf.
  6. Derive the initial water mass and condensed-steam/ice mass from the balance readings.
Instrument resolutions used in this simulation: balance 0.1 g; thermometer 0.1°C. The report should preserve sensible significant figures.
