Ideal-Gas Laws & Absolute Zero

Official USTHB gas-law practical: rapid syringe compression with pressure/temperature acquisition, dead-volume correction, a stepped equilibrium series, and absolute-zero determinations at constant and variable amounts of gas.

Data acquisitiondead volume V₀PVT verificationabsolute zero

Part 1 — ideal-gas apparatus

The published practical uses a syringe, thermistor and absolute-pressure sensor connected to acquisition software. The first manipulation starts from a syringe reading of 40 cc, uses a rapid compression toward the minimum volume, then reads pressure and temperature from the acquired traces.

P₁(V₁+V₀)=P₂(V₂+V₀)
PV/T = nR

V₀ is the dead volume of tubing/sensor connections and must be determined experimentally rather than ignored.

Part 2 — absolute zero

At constant volume, pressure is recorded while a sealed sphere is cooled in steps. A straight-line extrapolation of temperature versus pressure gives the pressure-zero temperature. The manual then repeats the experiment with three different bath orders so the amount of trapped gas changes between runs.

T°C = aP + T₀

Virtual apparatus calibration: sphere internal volume = 100.0 mL. This simulation label is supplied so the manual's requested mole calculations can be completed.
