Focometry

Determine focal lengths by the conjugate-points, Bessel, Silbermann and autocollimation methods, then verify the vergence law for attached lenses and infer a divergent-lens focal length.

Thin-lens relation

1/p′ − 1/p = 1/f′

With the USTHB sign convention, a real object has p<0 and a real image p′>0. In the conjugate-points method, several object/lens positions are used and 1/p′ is plotted against 1/p.

Methods in the manual

  • Conjugate points: repeated p, p′ and reciprocal graph.
  • Bessel: fixed object–screen distance D>4f and two sharp lens positions O₁, O₂.
  • Silbermann: reduce D until O₁ and O₂ merge; f=D₀/4.
  • Autocollimation: reflected image returns to the object plane when the object is at the focal plane.
  • Attached lenses: verify v=v₁+v₂ and infer a divergent lens.

Theoretical background

A thin converging lens obeys the conjugate relation 1/p′−1/p=1/f′, with transverse magnification γ=p′/p under the adopted sign convention. Different focometry methods exploit the same imaging law in different geometries and therefore have different dominant uncertainties.

For Bessel's method, if the object–screen separation is D and the two sharp lens positions are separated by d, f′=(D²−d²)/(4D). At the limiting Silbermann position d→0, f′=D₀/4. In autocollimation, coincidence of the returned image with the object occurs when the object lies in the focal plane. For thin lenses in contact, vergences add: 1/fe=1/f₁+1/f₂.
