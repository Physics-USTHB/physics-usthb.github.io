X-ray tube spectrum

The continuous bremsstrahlung spectrum has a short-wavelength cutoff controlled by tube voltage, while the target's characteristic line wavelengths remain essentially fixed.

λmin=hc/(eU)

Crystal spectrometer

A crystal of known plane spacing d converts wavelength to angle through first-order Bragg diffraction.

λ=2d sinθ
E=hc/λ

Record the detector count rate manually as the crystal/telescope angle is scanned. The software does not reveal λ or line identity at the cursor.

Theoretical background

Electrons accelerated through a tube voltage U can lose kinetic energy as bremsstrahlung. The maximum photon energy is eU, giving the short-wavelength cutoff λmin=hc/(eU). Superimposed characteristic lines arise from transitions between atomic shells of the target and therefore occur at target-specific energies.

The crystal spectrometer uses constructive interference from lattice planes. For first-order Bragg diffraction, λ=2d sinθ, and the photon energy then follows from E=hc/λ. Tube-voltage changes move the continuum cutoff but do not significantly shift the target's characteristic line wavelengths.
