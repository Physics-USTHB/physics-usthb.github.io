Poisson counting

Repeated counts made with identical geometry and equal counting time fluctuate statistically. For an ideal Poisson process:

Var(N) ≈ ⟨N⟩
σN ≈ √⟨N⟩

Counting time

For a fixed source rate, longer counting intervals increase the mean number of counts while reducing the fractional fluctuation.

σ/⟨N⟩ ≈ 1/√⟨N⟩

Acquire at least two ensembles at the same source setting but with different counting times.

Theoretical background

Radioactive decays are independent random events to a good approximation over ordinary laboratory counting intervals. If the mean expected count is μ, the number N recorded in one interval follows a Poisson distribution, P(N)=e−μμN/N!, with Var(N)=μ.

The statistical standard deviation is therefore approximately σ≈√N for a measured count N, while the fractional statistical uncertainty scales as 1/√N. Longer counting times collect more events and reduce relative random uncertainty, which is why counting-time choice is a central experimental variable rather than a cosmetic setting.
