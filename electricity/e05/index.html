RC series circuit

τRC = RTC,    RT = R + rg
VC(t) = E(1 − e−t/τ)   during charge

At (t= au), (V_Capprox0.63E). By (5 au), the capacitor is practically fully charged. During the second half-period it discharges exponentially.

RL series circuit

τRL = L/RT,    RT = R + rg + Rb
VR(t) = VR,max(1 − e−t/τ)

The voltage across R is proportional to current, so its rising transient is used to measure the current-establishment time constant.

The two graphical methods taught in the handout

4-square method

Adjust the final response to 6 vertical divisions. Take 4 divisions as the τ level. Since 4/6 ≈ 0.67 rather than 0.63, this method slightly overestimates τ.

5-square method

Adjust the final response to 8 vertical divisions. Take 5 divisions as the τ level. Since 5/8 = 0.625, this is much closer to the theoretical 0.63 level.

Measurement quality: the half-period must be long enough to show a complete transient (approximately (T/2ge5 au)), but τ must also occupy enough horizontal divisions to be read accurately.

Method bias

The graphical levels are approximations to the exact 63.2% time-constant level:

t(4/6) = −τ ln(1−4/6) ≈ 1.099τ
t(5/8) = −τ ln(1−5/8) ≈ 0.981τ

This explains why the handout describes the five-square method as more precise.
