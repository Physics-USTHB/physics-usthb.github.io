USTHB rheographic-tank protocol

The current USTHB course listing identifies this experiment as TP2. The virtual version preserves the published sequence: plane electrodes first, then the same geometry with a 6 cm conducting cylinder placed at the centre.

USTHB course listing ↗

Physical principle

The tank contains a weakly conducting liquid. Metallic electrodes are driven by an AC source to avoid electrode polarisation. A movable probe measures the local potential relative to electrode A.

dV = −E · dl

Therefore the electric field is normal to equipotential lines and directed toward decreasing potential.

E = −∇V

Official sequence

  1. Measure the potentials of electrodes A and B and verify symmetry about the x-axis.
  2. Map the 2 V, 4 V, 6 V and 8 V equipotentials.
  3. Use their x-axis intersections to estimate four mean field magnitudes.
  4. Measure V₁(x) every 2 cm along x′Ox and analyse field uniformity.
  5. Insert a 6 cm conducting cylinder at the centre and repeat the mapping.
  6. Trace field lines near y=+5 cm and y=−5 cm, then measure V₂(x) and compare with V₁(x).

Simulation geometry

The virtual tank uses a 20 cm electrode spacing with finite 16 cm electrode height inside a wider tank. The source is approximately 11 V AC. These dimensions are transparent simulation parameters chosen to reproduce the qualitative edge effects described in the handout.

Numerical model

The browser solves the two-dimensional Laplace equation on a rectangular finite-difference grid. Tank walls are treated as insulating boundaries; electrodes are fixed-potential boundaries. The central conductor is represented as a floating equipotential at the symmetry potential.

Important: students interact only with the virtual probe and measured potential. The full numerical field is not revealed until the relevant mapping task is completed.
