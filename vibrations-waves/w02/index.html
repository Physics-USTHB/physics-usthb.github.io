Koenig trombone / Quincke tube

Two coherent acoustic waves travel over different path lengths and interfere at the receiver. Moving the sliding U-tube by Δd changes the acoustic path difference by 2Δd.

ΔL = 2Δd,    v = λf

Successive amplitude maxima therefore satisfy D = λ/2 in the slider displacement.

USTHB measurements

  1. Fix f between 4.5 and 5.5 kHz; scan Δd from 0 to about 15 cm in 0.5 cm steps and plot receiver amplitude.
  2. Read the displacement period D and calculate v=2fD.
  3. Vary f from 1 to 6 kHz and manually locate two successive maxima for each frequency.
  4. Plot D versus 1/f; obtain v from twice the fitted slope and estimate its uncertainty from the scatter.

Theoretical background

Two coherent sound waves arriving at the receiver with path difference ΔL have phase difference Δφ=2πΔL/λ. Their amplitudes reinforce for ΔL=mλ and oppose for half-integer multiples of λ. The measured receiver amplitude therefore oscillates as the path difference is changed.

In the Koenig/Quincke geometry, moving the sliding section by Δd changes the total acoustic path difference by 2Δd. Successive maxima in slider position are consequently separated by D=λ/2. Combining λ=2D with v=fλ gives the sound speed from either one frequency or from the slope of D versus 1/f.
