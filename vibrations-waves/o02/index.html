Measurement programme

  1. Scan 1–12 kHz for R=0 Ω and R=100 Ω, with extra points around the resonance and half-power crossings.
  2. Plot V₀(f) and φ(f), locate fr, Vmax, f₁ and f₂.
  3. Calculate Δf=f₂−f₁, Q=fr/Δf and compare with Vmax/e₀.
  4. For the unknown specimen, use the −90° phase crossing to determine f₀ and then infer Cₓ and Rₓ using the L and Rᵣ obtained in Practical 1.

Theoretical background

A sinusoidally driven damped oscillator reaches a steady-state response at the driving frequency, but with an amplitude and phase that depend strongly on frequency. Near resonance the response amplitude becomes large; damping broadens the resonance peak and reduces its maximum.

The phase changes continuously through the resonance region, and the bandwidth between the half-power frequencies provides an experimental measure of damping. For a sufficiently sharp resonance, Q≈fr/Δf. Measuring both amplitude and phase is important because the resonance frequency and the undamped natural frequency need not be identical when damping is appreciable.
