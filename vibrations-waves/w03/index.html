Theoretical background

A pulse travelling a known distance Δx in time Δt has speed v=Δx/Δt. In a fluid, sound is a longitudinal pressure wave and its speed is related to the adiabatic bulk modulus B and density ρ by v=√(B/ρ), so B=ρv².

Solids support both longitudinal and transverse elastic waves. At a fluid–solid interface, Snell's law relates propagation angles and speeds; at the transverse critical angle the refracted transverse ray reaches 90°, giving the relation used to infer VT. Once VL, VT and density are known, an isotropic elastic model gives Young's modulus. Temperature changes the fluid sound speed because density and compressibility both vary.
