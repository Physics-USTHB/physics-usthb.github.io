Ultrasonic Diffraction

Measure diffraction maxima of a pulsed 40 kHz ultrasonic wave, determine the acoustic wavelength and sound speed, and verify the grating law from a multi-order angular scan.

40 kHz ultrasoundgrating diffractionangular scan → wavelength → sound speed

Objective

A 40 kHz ultrasonic transmitter illuminates a grating. Rotate the receiver and locate the central and diffracted intensity maxima. For each non-zero order, measure the positive and negative angular positions rather than relying on a single side.

d sin θm = mλ

Use the mean magnitude of the symmetric angles to calculate λ, then determine the sound speed from c=fλ.

Experimental discipline

  1. Use transmitter synchronisation so the received pulse is stable.
  2. Choose a pulse period long enough for the emitted/received packets to be separated.
  3. Scan through an expected peak and centre it from the receiver amplitude—not from a hidden angle.
  4. Measure both +m and −m for each order.
  5. Repeat across several grating spacings and orders.
  6. Finish with a graph of sin θm versus m/d; its slope is λ.

What the virtual apparatus does—and does not do

The simulator generates a finite-width ultrasonic diffraction signal with peak broadening and small repeatable readout variation. It does not reveal the theoretical peak angles. The student must locate maxima, identify orders, calculate wavelength and sound speed, and analyse the grating law.
