Propagation of Waves Along a Coaxial Line

USTHB 2022 transmission-line laboratory practical: progressive and reflected waves, propagation speed, reflection coefficient, attenuation, characteristic impedance, normal modes and fault location.

USTHB coaxial-line apparatus

The line consists of two 100 m cable coils connected in series, giving X = 200 m. A signal generator and oscilloscope are used in pulse and sinusoidal regimes with open, short-circuit, matched and variable terminal loads.

Required practical work

  1. Identify incident/reflected pulses; measure round-trip delay and propagation speed.
  2. Measure attenuation at 0, 100, 200, 300 and 400 m equivalent path lengths.
  3. Measure |Γ| versus variable terminal resistance, infer Zc, and compare with theory.
  4. Use sinusoidal propagation to determine delay/speed and short/open normal-mode frequencies.
  5. Measure open/short input impedances near 1 MHz and obtain Zc.
  6. Use pulse reflections from both ends of a faulty line to identify and locate two defects.

Theoretical background

A transmission line has distributed inductance L′ and capacitance C′, so voltage and current propagate as waves rather than appearing everywhere simultaneously. In the low-loss approximation, v≈1/√(L′C′) and Zc≈√(L′/C′). A load different from Zc reflects part of the incident wave with coefficient Γ=(ZT−Zc)/(ZT+Zc).

Pulse delay measures propagation speed directly, while amplitude decay is modelled approximately by A(x)=A₀e−αx. In sinusoidal operation, incident and reflected waves form standing-wave patterns whose allowed modes depend on open or short termination. Reflections from an impedance discontinuity return after a round trip, so its distance is obtained from d=vΔt/2.
