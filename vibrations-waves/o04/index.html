Two forced coupled oscillators

The two identical RLC loops are coupled by C₀ and driven sinusoidally. The ideal mode frequencies obey Ω₁²=1/(LC) and Ω₂²=(1+2C/C₀)/(LC). Measure the amplitudes V₀₁ and V₀₂ versus frequency. The response displays two proper resonances; V₁ also has an antiresonance between them.

K = (ω₂²−ω₁²)/(ω₂²+ω₁²)

USTHB measurement sequence

  1. For one C₀, scan 1–18 kHz and add fine points near both resonance peaks and the V₁ antiresonance.
  2. From the measured curves obtain f₁, fmin, f₂ and K.
  3. Repeat for at least three coupling capacitors.
  4. Plot ω₁² and ω₂² against K and interpret how coupling separates the two proper frequencies.

Theoretical background

When the coupled system is driven sinusoidally, the forcing excites both normal coordinates. The steady-state frequency response therefore contains two resonance regions associated with the two proper modes. Their separation grows as coupling becomes stronger.

Between the two resonances, one measured coordinate can pass through an antiresonance: contributions from the two modal responses cancel so that the observed amplitude becomes very small even though the system is being driven. Resonance positions reveal the normal-mode frequencies, while the antiresonance is evidence of destructive superposition in the coupled response.
