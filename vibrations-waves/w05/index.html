Loaded-tube sequence

  1. Scan roughly 100 Hz–10 kHz in free field and determine the impedance resonance f₀ and low-frequency resistance.
  2. Set the generator to f₀ and install the source/receiver in the tube.
  3. Move the receiver and identify in-phase, opposition and quadrature positions from the two acoustic traces.
  4. Use successive in-phase positions to find λ and v=f₀λ.
  5. At a quadrature acoustic position, switch back to MATH subtraction and measure the loaded loudspeaker impedance and its electrical phase.

Theoretical background

A loudspeaker is an electromechanical resonator, so its electrical input impedance depends on frequency. Around the mechanical resonance, energy exchange between the moving diaphragm and the electrical circuit produces a pronounced impedance feature. Measuring both |Z| and phase distinguishes the resistive and reactive parts of the response.

In the acoustic tube, a sinusoidal pressure wave accumulates phase as it propagates: Δφ=2πd/λ. Successive receiver positions with the same phase are separated by one wavelength, so v=fλ. Loading the loudspeaker with the tube changes the acoustic reaction on the diaphragm and therefore changes its electrical input impedance, linking the acoustic and electrical parts of the practical.
