Electrical analogue of a damped oscillator

The series RLC circuit is the electrical analogue of a mass–spring–damper. The capacitor voltage VC(t) plays the role of displacement. The excitation is a 0.4 V square signal at a frequency no higher than 100 Hz.

δ = RT/(2L),    ω₀ = 1/√(LC)

The known capacitor is C = 0.470 µF. The circuit also contains a small residual series resistance that must be inferred from the damped response.

Core tasks

  1. With the known circuit, increase the adjustable R until the boundary between oscillatory and aperiodic response is reached; record RC.
  2. Return R to zero and use same-sign extrema separated by several periods to measure Ta and the logarithmic decrement D.
  3. Deduce δ, Q, L and the residual resistance Rr.
  4. Insert the unknown Rx/Cx specimen, measure D′ and T′a, then determine Rx and Cx.

Theoretical background

The free response of a series RLC circuit obeys the same second-order form as a damped mechanical oscillator: x¨+2δx˙+ω₀²x=0, with the capacitor voltage playing the role of displacement. Here δ=RT/(2L) and ω₀=1/√(LC).

If δ<ω₀ the response is oscillatory with an exponentially decaying envelope; at critical damping δ=ω₀ the return to equilibrium is fastest without oscillation; for larger damping the response is aperiodic. In the underdamped case, the logarithmic decrement between equal-phase extrema gives the decay rate, while the quality factor is approximately Q=ω₀/(2δ) for weak damping.

Realise the source and RLC montage first

The USTHB practical begins by realising a low-output-resistance generator, then using it to excite the series RLC circuit. Virtual-bench values: GBF internal resistance 50 Ω, RAC=100 Ω and RAB=10 Ω.

Build the low-resistance source and the series RLC measurement circuit before energising it.

Place both cursors on same-sign extrema. Read each peak's vertical deviation from the final plateau directly from the graticule; do not use the generator setting as an amplitude measurement.

Start with the known circuit. Adjust R until the oscillatory overshoot just disappears, then record the critical setting.
