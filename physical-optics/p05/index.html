Diffraction by Slits

Curriculum-aligned Fraunhofer single- and double-slit measurements with hidden aperture dimensions, direct screen readings and student recovery of slit width and separation.

Single slit

For slit width a, minima satisfy a sinθ=mλ. In the small-angle screen geometry, the first minimum is approximately:

x₁ ≈ λD/a

Measure x₁ from the central maximum to the first dark minimum and calculate the hidden slit width.

Double slit

A double slit combines the same diffraction envelope with fine interference fringes. Measure a span of ten fine fringes to recover the centre-to-centre slit separation d:

i ≈ λD/d

The source wavelength is known: 632.8 nm.

Theoretical background

Fraunhofer diffraction from a single slit of width a has minima where a sinθ=mλ for non-zero integer m. The central maximum is therefore wider when the slit is narrower. In the small-angle screen geometry, position is related to angle by x≈Dθ.

A double slit produces fine two-source interference fringes inside a broader single-slit diffraction envelope. The fine-fringe spacing is approximately i=λD/d, where d is the centre-to-centre slit separation, while the envelope is controlled by a. Observing both scales in the same pattern separates the two geometrical quantities.

Fraunhofer diffraction bench

Start with the single slit: position cursor x₁ on the first dark minimum, read the ruler and calculate a. In double-slit mode also use both cursors on bright fringes ten spacings apart.
