Polarisation of Light

Curriculum-aligned experimental study of analyser transmission, Malus' law, crossed polarisers and quarter-wave-plate polarisation states.

Polariser and analyser

For linearly polarised light incident on an analyser at relative angle θ, the transmitted intensity follows Malus' law. A real detector retains a small background signal, so the experimental fit is allowed a non-zero intercept.

I(θ)=Ibg+I₀ cos²θ

Measure a broad analyser scan with the quarter-wave plate removed. The graph and fit are built only from readings you record.

Quarter-wave plate

A λ/4 plate introduces a π/2 phase delay between its principal axes. With the plate axes at 45° to the incoming linear polarisation, the two orthogonal components are equal and the ideal output is circularly polarised.

Insert the plate, set the 45° condition and scan the analyser again. Circular light should show very weak analyser-angle modulation instead of crossed-polariser extinction.

Theoretical background

Polarisation describes the direction and phase relation of the transverse electric-field components of light. A linear polariser transmits the field component along its transmission axis; a second polariser therefore gives Malus' law, I=I₀cos²θ in the ideal case.

A quarter-wave plate introduces a relative phase shift of π/2 between field components along its fast and slow axes. If an incident linear polarisation is at 45° to those axes, the two components have equal amplitudes and emerge in quadrature, producing circular polarisation ideally. An analyser scan distinguishes linear, elliptical and nearly circular states through the depth of intensity modulation.
