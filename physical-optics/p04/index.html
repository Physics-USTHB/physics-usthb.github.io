Newton Rings

Measure dark-ring diameters and use the D²–order relation to determine the wavelength or lens curvature.

USTHB curriculum topicmeasurement-firstwave-optics analysis

Newton-ring geometry

A plano-convex lens resting on a plane glass plate creates a thin air film. In reflected monochromatic light the dark-ring diameters obey:

Dₘ² = 4 m λ R

Measurement method

Focus the travelling microscope on the ring plane. Measure left and right positions for several dark rings; use the diameter rather than a radius to reduce centring error. Plot Dₘ² against order m.

Theoretical background

Newton rings are thin-film interference fringes produced by the air gap between a spherical lens and a plane plate. Near the contact point the film thickness varies approximately as t≈r²/(2R). In reflected light one of the interfering reflections undergoes a π phase reversal, giving a dark centre for an ideal contact.

For dark rings at normal incidence, rm²=mλR, or equivalently Dm²=4mλR. Plotting squared diameter against order produces a straight line and avoids relying on the exact visual location of the centre.
