Michelson Interferometer

Air plate — equal inclination

With the two effective mirrors parallel, Michelson fringes are circular equal-inclination fringes localised at infinity. They are observed in the image focal plane of a converging lens.

δ = 2e cos i

Air wedge — equal thickness

A small angle between the effective mirrors produces straight equal-thickness fringes localised near the mirrors.

δ = 2αx

The two configurations therefore require different mirror geometry and localisation choices.

Theoretical background

A Michelson interferometer divides one beam into two optical paths and recombines them. Interference depends on the optical path difference between the two returning beams. When the effective mirrors are parallel, rays emerging at the same inclination have the same path difference, producing circular equal-inclination fringes described by δ=2e cos i.

If one effective mirror is slightly tilted, the air layer behaves as a wedge: thickness changes approximately linearly with position and the fringes become nearly straight, equal-thickness fringes. Translating a mirror changes the round-trip path by twice the mirror displacement, so fringe counting can be used for precise displacement or wavelength measurements.
