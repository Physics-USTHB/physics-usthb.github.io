Graphical Study of Circular Motion

Vector and intrinsic-coordinate analysis of a circular recording produced by an electric hammer bell at 0.01 s intervals: displacement, mean and instantaneous velocity, ΔV, acceleration, tangential/normal components and V(t).

USTHB handout workflowcalibrated graphical worksheetbrowser-local progress

Official objective

Carry out vector and intrinsic-coordinate studies of a circular motion, using position, displacement, velocity and acceleration vectors and their evolution with time. The emphasis is graphical method, vector construction, graphing, analysis and interpretation.

The recording is analysed with:

δts = 0.01 s,    Δte = 8δts = 0.08 s

Positions P₀, P₁, … are therefore separated by 0.08 s on the analysis sheet.

USTHB tasks reproduced

  1. Construct successive displacement vectors Δrᵢ from P₀ onward.
  2. Determine mean-speed magnitudes and choose a graphical velocity scale.
  3. Verify at Pi3 (t = 0.20 s) that the mean velocity can represent instantaneous velocity tangent to the path.
  4. At P₂, P₃, P₄ and P₅ construct ΔV, then a = ΔV/Δte.
  5. Resolve acceleration into tangential and normal components.
  6. Plot V(t), identify the nature of the motion, and compare at with dV/dt and an with V²/R.

Inclined board, tether and hammer bell

Prepare the carbon-paper recording, keep the string taut, energise the hammer bell, then release it from rest at P₀.

Experimental principle

The smooth metal disk carries an electric hammer bell. Its hammer passes through the disk and marks the carbon-paper sheet every 0.01 s. The inextensible string remains taut to nail O, constraining the disk to a circular path.
