Friction & Contact Force

Determine the dynamic-friction coefficient for a cart on a horizontal table from hammer-bell kinematics and Newton's second law, then repeat with a 1 kg overload to test whether the coefficient depends on mass.

USTHB official workflowscale-1 ticker stripkinematics → force decomposition → mass test

Official apparatus and sequence

A 1.6 kg cart moves on a horizontal table. It is attached to an elastic band of unstretched length l₀ and carries a paper strip recorded by an electric hammer bell every 0.01 s. Initially the elastic is stretched; at time tₑ it returns to l₀ and then becomes slack.

The hammer interaction with the paper produces an approximately constant resisting force of 0.3 N throughout the motion.

Work required

  1. Mark every 8th impact, measure successive Δx and calculate V₁ at midpoint times using Δtₑ=0.08 s.
  2. Plot V₁(t) at the prescribed scales: 1 cm ↔ 0.05 s and 1 cm ↔ 0.1 m/s.
  3. Identify motion phases, tₑ, final-phase acceleration a₁, final speed V(tf), and l₀; compare l₀ with the direct recording measurement.
  4. Use P + C + f = Ma₁ to analyse the table contact C, derive μd₁ and decide whether C depends on speed.
  5. At maximum speed, determine the elastic tension Tₑ.
  6. Plot the supplied overloaded-cart data V₂(t), determine a₂, calculate μd₂ and compare μd₁ with μd₂.
