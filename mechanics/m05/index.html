MECHANICS · L1 SM/ST · LABORATORY PRACTICAL 5

Energy Transfer

Study a coupled two-body system on a 60° incline crossing two contact surfaces and then stretching a spring. Process the hammer-bell record, determine friction coefficients and spring force, and track kinetic, gravitational and elastic energies.

USTHB current handout configuration4/50 s kinematic analysisdynamics + energy accounting

Official configuration

Body A, M₁ = 510 g, starts at the bottom of a plane inclined by α = 60°. It is connected by an inextensible string over a pulley to body B, M₂ = 580 g, and also to a spring of stiffness k = 9 N/m. The spring acts only once stretched.

A moves first on surface S₁, then S₂. A hammer bell fixed at the top records the motion through a paper strip attached to B at 1/50 s = 0.02 s intervals. The first impacts are intentionally omitted; the supplied record begins at P₀(t₀,x₀).

Required USTHB work

  1. Process the scale-1 record with Δtₑ = 4/50 s = 0.08 s and construct V(t−t₀) using 1 cm ↔ 0.08 s and 1 cm ↔ 0.05 m/s.
  2. Determine accelerations a₁ and a₂ in the first two motion phases.
  3. With hammer drag f = 0.3 N and g = 10 m/s², determine μg₁ and μg₂ and discuss speed dependence.
  4. At t₁=t₀+1.50 s, determine spring force and represent forces on A and B at 1 cm ↔ 1 N.
  5. At P₀…P₅ determine V and curvilinear abscissa s; calculate total kinetic, both gravitational and elastic energies.
  6. Plot ET(s), explain its shape and what non-conservative work can be deduced.
